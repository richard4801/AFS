How to Get Paid to Write Fiction Online

Serialised fiction platforms pay thousands of writers to publish stories chapter by chapter. How much you earn depends less on how fast you type and more on how the deal is set up. This guide explains the common options and what to check before you sign.

How serialised fiction works

On serialised platforms, readers pay to unlock chapters as they're released. A book that keeps readers coming back for hundreds of chapters can earn for years. Most of this income goes to whoever owns the story, so who owns your book matters.

The two common ways writers are paid

Flat rate per word

You're paid a fixed amount for every word you deliver. Income is predictable, but it stops when you stop writing. If the book becomes a hit, the extra money goes to the publisher.

Royalty share

You receive a percentage of what the book earns. Early months may be smaller, but a book that performs keeps paying. The percentage, and whether it's taken from gross or net revenue, makes a big difference.

What to check before you sign

  • Gross or net. A share of gross revenue is taken before costs. A share of net can shrink after deductions you can't see.
  • How long you're paid. Check whether royalties continue for the full contract or stop after a set period.
  • Visibility. Ask whether you can see your book's earnings and reads, or whether you only receive a monthly figure.
  • Rights and pen names. Check who owns the story and whether you can write under a private pen name.
  • Bonuses and payout rules. Look at minimum payout thresholds and when bonuses are paid.
